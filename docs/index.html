    Method

    Three rules, fixed before any result

    01

    Only what's known the night before

    The offer, the filed range (read from the last S-1/A on EDGAR before the first trade), deal size, venue, and a 90-day read of how recent IPOs traded. A leakage check raises on anything else.

    02

    A band calibrated out of sample

    Gradient-boosted 10th, 50th and 90th percentile models of the first-day move, then widened on a later, separate window (conformal calibration). That makes 80% coverage hold on average across listings, provided the future resembles the calibration window. It promises nothing for any one slice, as the track record shows.

    03

    A gate that says no

    No filed range, fewer than five recent IPOs, or a band wider than 60% of the offer: any one of these and the number is withheld. The gate never narrows a band; it only decides whether to show one.

    Track record

    Scored on 428 IPOs it never saw

    Trained on 2019–2022, calibrated on 2023, scored from January 2024 to October 2026. The rule for winning was written down first: be narrower than a simple benchmark at the same coverage.

    Verdict: the benchmark stands. Across every listing the model's band is twice as wide. Most of that is micro-caps, whose first days the deal facts can't predict. Its median misses by 22.6% of the offer on average, against 23.6% for "it closes at the offer".

    Where the gate publishes, the band is a quarter narrower, but it caught only 68% of outcomes against an 80% target. An earlier, better result on this subset did not replicate. Read a published band as tighter than the evidence supports. The benchmark is the grey band in the calculator: the offer times the usual spread of first-day moves.

    The live calculator uses the same method refitted on more recent data: trained through 2023 and calibrated on 2024 to October 2026. The table above scores the method; the ANT number is the refit's.
